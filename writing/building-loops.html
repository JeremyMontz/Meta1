Building loops for an AI-enabled PM toolchain.

One PM, a team-shaped system, and all the hats.

Everyone in the AI community is talking about loops right now: agents that run, check their own work, and run again without a human turning the crank. I set out to learn how loops work in Claude Code and Cowork, and what they could do for me.

The thing is, I already work in loops. They just have me orchestrating everything, slowing the process down. And every task an AI finishes for me comes back with two more for the backlog. It was growing faster than I could work it. I needed the agent corps to take on tasks without me, autonomously, with my judgment applied at a few deliberate gates instead of at every step. I needed a pipeline.

The first lane was self-evident. Three months of rapid building had left a backlog of untested work, and everything I ship from here forward needs tests to maintain site stability. So test-case creation will be the first skill and workflow pilot. Next, a builder skill to do the code work, then a verification and review workflow to adjudicate or escalate. I configured GitHub to hold the hard gates, and gave Claudemonzter a bot account so the agents act under their own permissions, signed and auditable. Twenty years of product management taught me the shape of this arrangement long before AI made it possible: write the requirement precisely, gate what ships, own the judgment calls.

Wearing many hats has always been the product manager’s job. AI enables me to put on two new ones: builder and tester.

My actual workflow reaches wider than any one platform. Claude.ai’s design mode produced the site’s design system and still inspires new pages; ChatGPT draws the imagery. This coming month I’m focusing on what fits inside Cowork, so I cut scope accordingly. (Cutting scope is the job, even for personal projects.) The vision is compelling: one PM operating a team-shaped agentic system, modeled on every human team I have worked with, except now I am the sole operator, every hat on at once.

So that is the roadmap for month four: build the pipeline, run the practice deliberately, and write down what it produces. This factory is my most ambitious project yet, agents on the assembly line and my hands on the gates, a system that helps build itself. The next few field notes will walk through what comes out of it, including the parts that fail. Especially those parts.
