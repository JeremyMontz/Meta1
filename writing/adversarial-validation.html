Adversarial validation and structured perspective expansion.

Multiple personalities, on purpose.

I built one to challenge me and keep the brown-nosing in check. Then I read about a way to invoke a council of five personas cleverly coordinating on an answer to the user’s question. I also learned the BMAD development framework scales to ten. What they share in common aren’t necessarily “agents” but valuable abstractions you can build working software with.

The first one was Assessor. I built it to assess me and the Evolve curriculum about AI I’m learning from. Its directive was clear: honest evaluation, no inflating my self-worth. Evolve, the coach and mentor, builds me up and Assessor demands evidence of concepts learned or demonstrated. It has its own rubric, its own way to score the student. I had to build some containment rules so it wasn’t biased by prior work product, and to keep the teaching from knowing how the tests work. Anti-sycophancy was the intent but the structure delivered real differences and insights, proof the concept was working.

Around the same time I read about Andrej Karpathy’s LLM Council: five model instances stress-testing a question from different angles, critiquing each other, synthesizing a verdict. I built a version of the skill inside claude.ai. Unlike the original, it runs the five lenses sequentially in a single context window, so later advisors can be tainted by earlier framing. Advisor order matters now: First Principles opens the original question, Contrarian stress-tests the previous positions, with a Chairman synthesizing the strengths and weaknesses of the positions. The session ends with a Context Audit naming which documents informed the opinion, and suggesting other research that would inform a future council round. And although this downsized, sequential version of the skill is less ‘pure’ than the original’s true architectural independence, it can still produce startling insights into the questions you throw at it.

Some minds carry domain context. Others wear adversarial postures. Some split and recombine for analysis. All of them are testing something. Is “agent” the right word for any of it?

I later extended the same pattern to the development side of Claudemonzter with Bond, a quality control agent that tests what Meta1 produces. The scale isn’t the most interesting part. What’s interesting to me is what are we calling an “agent”? Assessor and Bond are defined by their context. The claude.ai council members are five personae appearing into any context, providing an opinion within a single session, and then exiting so that the previous persona can resume, and even add new commentary on top of that. The shapes differ; each probes something the agent next to it can’t answer alone. Maybe “agent” isn’t the right word for any of it.
