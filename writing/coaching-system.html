Building a closed-loop adaptive coaching system.

Teaching Claude to teach me how to use itself.

I wanted a coaching system that got smarter with me. A signal from one agent passed through the orchestrator could change how every other agent guided me when prompted. Closed-loop, adaptive, cross-context.

The pieces were being built in tandem and from scratch — Evolve teaching and scoring me, the idea being that if other agents knew my proficiency level (or lack thereof) they’d shift their register and preemptively expand on topics I didn’t yet know. I later split the testing off from the teaching into a separate agent — Assessor — to keep the scoring honest. The proficiency score lived at a designated layer of the load order so every agent could read it at session start. This worked. The roadmap had us researching what aspects of Claude could be ‘dialed-in’ and made coach-like on demand to interrogate a question. What I was truly after was cross-agent behavior change triggered by a skill invocation — a specific use case and a POC of a universal pattern. Something I could actually build, despite having no prior AI experience.

The use case was to help me as a beginner. The capability and the learning were universal.

I had big dreams for The Coach. But the online platform didn’t quite ground the design. Around the same time I noticed Claude.ai added a register-shifting feature — Styles, including one called Learning — that I’d been reinventing the hard way. I shifted to Cowork and substantiated the persona matrix there — nine behavioral dials, like verbosity and register, that can be tuned in an online portal, with a skill that cascades changes across the graph. The concepts that began with the Coach now make up Claudemonzter’s ‘spirit,’ which I’ll elaborate on in a future piece.

Filed under field notes. The persona engine these concepts became lives in the spirit.
