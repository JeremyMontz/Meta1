Proportionate security.

A courtesy lock for the curious.

Near the end of my third month I wanted to start showing what I'd built around. A few of my portal pages still wrote straight to the backend, and I needed to close an open door. Anyone poking around could mess with my live data.

My architect agent wanted a proper, secure, robust, complicated solution. I didn't. I was on a deadline and had other priorities. I was not guarding secrets against a determined hacker. This was a public lab that just needed to stop curious onlookers clicking around from tripping me up. At least those were the people I hoped would come visit. A real lock that stops the obvious path is not "nothing," whatever my agent would have me believe.

The strongest control is rarely the right one. Don't let agents set the agenda.

So I made the cut. Basic security shipped and did what the minimum; the bigger fix went to the backlog, scoped as its own story for a day when I can give it the attention it deserves. Nothing was lost, only scheduled. Overruling the architect, for now, was the right product judgment call: knowing what to ship now, and what to scope for later.
